# Introduction to Syriac Verbs[^1]
Verbs in Classical Syriac are formed by applying different vowels, prefixes, and/or suffixes to the **verbal root.**

Most often, a root consists of 3 "radicals"(consonants).[^2] 

### Ex: The root for ܩܛܠܠ, 'he killed'

Verbal inflections can tell us the 1) conjugation, 2) tense, and 3) person, number, and gender of a given verb. 

## Conjugations
There are six main conjugations in Syriac, which are based off of the verb ܦܥܠܠ, which means 'he labours.' The six forms are as follows: 

| Conjugation | Description       |
| ----------- | ----------------- |
| Pe'al       | Simple Form       |
| Ethpe'el    | Passive Simple    |
| Pa'el       | Intensive Form    |
| Ethpa'al    | Passive Intensive |
| Aph;el      | Causative Form    |
| Ettaph'al   | Passive Causative |

## Tense
There are two tenses in Syriac, which do not work in the same way as English. 

1. Perfect
2. Imperfect
#### Perfect
The perfect tense communicates simple past action. "I read," "you made," "we jumped," etc. 

#### Imperfect
The imperfect tense often communicates future action. "I will read," "you will make," "we will jump," etc.
